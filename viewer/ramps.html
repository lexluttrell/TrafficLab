How the colors work

Local = same road and direction, in 100 m sections, at the current simulation sample. Gray roads have no cars. Relative colors compare each car with the other cars; fewer than two peers stays gray. These are simulated speeds.

Do the ramps help?
The acceleration-lane case uses the central ramp demand and adds two assumed 150 m acceleration lanes. The first four cases retain the original expanded network. All share identical mainline departure schedules and the same 18-bin scoring mask. Low/high cases vary inferred exits; they are assumptions, not confidence intervals. Driver parameters are unchanged. These scores are not directly comparable with Mission 003’s older score.

Did the requested traffic enter?
Scheduled vehicles are prescribed demand. Inserted vehicles joined before 08:30; completed vehicles reached a model boundary before 08:30. The extra drain period adds no new demand and checks that every remaining trip finishes. No route is artificially looped back into the corridor.
What is measured, and what is assumed?
Exit routing is assigned to source departure bins; cars reach ramps later. Hour-total balance ignores changing vehicle storage and travel-time offsets. Ramp endpoints are model boundaries, not surveyed detector positions. No ramp metering or external arterial queue is imposed.
MISSION 006 · TEST THE ASSUMPTION
Does it still work if the lanes are shorter?
We tested 100, 150 and 200 m acceleration-lane settings with two random seeds each. A seed changes random departure timing, exit assignments and driver behavior. The same traffic counts, inferred exits and driver parameters are used throughout. Within each seed, departure times are identical across lengths. Neither length nor seed was selected to minimize error.
Loading experiment results…
 
Two seeds are a limited sensitivity check, not a statistical confidence interval. Scores use the same 18 fully observed eastbound station-bins; there is no independent westbound score. Trip times include completed trips from the drain period. Stopped time is SUMO’s in-network waiting-time measure, excluding time before insertion; unfinished trips would be excluded from these percentiles. A lower error here does not establish the correct physical lane length.

The boundary still matters
Westbound observations show a slowdown toward the western edge: at 08:00, station 400301 reports 41.6 mph (100% observed), 401480 reports 44.3 mph (75%), and farther west 400538 reports 25.7 mph (75%, ambiguous mapping). This is evidence to investigate, not proof of an external cause. The model ends before the Richmond ramps and imposes no downstream queue.
